WHAT CHANGED FROM V1

The GPT model stayed the same.

The prompt now requires hierarchical classification, evidence handling, eleven fields, and confidence preservation. Count, output ceiling, and allocation also changed.

WHAT WORKED / WHAT FAILED

Processing passed. Semantic acceptance did not.

No 429s and valid JSON show the transport completed. They do not prove the model selected the correct routes, causes, or confidence values.

All 39 responses were schema-valid. All three acceptance gates failed.

Only 1 of 13 records per mode met every strict gold-field and rule check. Errors included incorrect secondary intents, cause extraction, and confidence preservation. Expected answers and thresholds were not changed to make the experiment pass.

What the prompt asks the model to do

Disambiguate hierarchical intents, ignore menu noise, honor corrections, extract distinct secondary topics, preserve supported facts, suppress synthetic identifier canaries, and apply archived-confidence rules.

Thirteen cases cover thirteen scenario families, not every route. The strict exact-match rubric is not a general model-quality score; newly generated free-form explanations are not independently evaluated for meaning.

Why this matters

Removing throttling did not remove analytical errors. A production workload needs both performance measurement and its own accuracy evaluation.

The model build is the same as v1; cache state, request sizes, allocation, and grading task differ. The 150K service allocation was not retested in the v2 comparison. Single runs do not establish that concurrency causes a quality difference.
